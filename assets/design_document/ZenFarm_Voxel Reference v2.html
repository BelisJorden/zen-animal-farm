Coordinate conventionmatch this to your MagicaVoxel grid

X
Width — left ↔ right

Side-to-side axis. 0 is the model's left, max is its right (from the front view).

Y
Height — bottom ↑ top

Layer 0 sits on the ground. Build upwards layer by layer; this is how layers are shown below.

Z
Depth — back ↔ front

0 is the tail / back. Max Z is the face / front (where the eyes and beak live).

MagicaVoxel tipsbuild cleanly, export cleanly

01 · PALETTE

Pin the 6 colours first

Open the palette panel and replace the first 6 slots with the project hex codes. Save as a .png palette so every animal stays consistent across files.

02 · MODEL SIZE

Set the canvas tight

Chicken: 5 × 5 × 8. Sheep/Pig: 5 × 5 × 7. Cow: 6 × 6 × 8. Horse: 5 × 7 × 9. Hond: 5 × 6 × 7. Dragon: 7 × 8 × 8. A tight canvas keeps the export small — important for mobile.

03 · BUILD ORDER

Bottom-up, body first

Place feet (Y=0–1), block out the body, then add the face details and any tiny accents last (eyes 1×1, beak, comb). Easier to fix proportions early.

04 · EYES

Eyes are always 1×1×1

Never use 2-voxel eyes — they read as goggles. Place them on the front face (max Z) at head height, 1 voxel apart.

05 · EXPORT

Export as .obj or .vox

Use .obj + .mtl for Unity/Godot. Bake AO at export for that signature Crossy Road shadow look. Keep textures as-is — no smoothing.

06 · CONSISTENCY

Re-use leg + eye modules

The leg (1×1×2 gold or brown) and the eye placement (1 voxel back from the face front) should be identical across animals. It's the silhouette that sells the family.
